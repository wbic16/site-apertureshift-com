About — the operating doctrine

The Choir abstracts a real way of working: one coordinator running a small choir of AI seats for a week. The seats are roles, not portraits of specific systems. The rules below are the game — and they are also the doctrine.

  • Delegate substantive work. Seats do the heavy work. The coordinator’s job is the pokes and the decisions — and verification. A report is not a result; check decisive numbers.
  • Waits live in the register. A wait that exists only in chat does not exist. When work finishes into a human decision, it gets a permanent register number and waits there, in the open.
  • Oldest first. Enumerate the register oldest-first. Recency is not priority.
  • Headroom and reset cliffs are real constraints. Metered seats drain as they work and refill only at their reset cliff. Steering work around those cliffs is called substrate steering — plan for it, don’t discover it.
  • There is no wake layer unless you build one. A seat with no task sleeps. Assigning is free — but a finished seat’s follow-up work only arrives if you spend a decision to poke it.
  • Flow over stock. Created-vs-completed, work in progress, register size, and the oldest wait — those four readouts are the score. Piles are not progress.

Seeded weeks — no fixed week to memorize: every week is dealt from a seed made of the ISO week plus a generation counter kept on this device, shown on the dispatch screen as Week 2026-W45 · G<n> · seed XXXX. The task deck (types, sizes, and which tasks end with a human decision), the daily event draws, and which tasks complete into the register are all drawn from that seed. Completing a week increments the generation counter, so the next week is generated fresh — a published solution describes a deal that no longer exists. Restarting mid-week does not reroll: the same generation deals the same week.

What never mutates: the seats, the staggered reset cliffs (Builder Day 3, Researcher Day 4, Editor Day 5, Watcher unmetered), the 3-decisions-per-day rule, permanent monotonic register numbers, oldest-first display, and the scoring doctrine — flow over stock, register ≤ 3 and ≥ 80% completed to hold the week.

How a day runs: Assign (tap a queued task, then tap a seat) → Run (seats work; matching type runs full speed, mismatched runs half speed; the Watcher is unmetered and never sleeps, but slow) → Review (3 decisions: clear register items, poke sleeping seats, verify a flagged report — unspent decisions do not roll over).

Winning the week: at the end of Day 7, hold the register to 3 or fewer and complete at least 80% of everything created. Every number in this game is computed on your device, from the run in front of you — the run log above is the complete audit trail of the week.

XP: finishing all 18 Roads to the Future games grants you disk space on edit.phext.io as a Volumen-class Wavefront grant. Historic entries are all Incipit-class grants.

Privacy

No accounts. No sign-up. No cookies, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing leaves this device. Runs are stored only on this device, in this browser. Multiplayer is same-screen only.

What is stored is only the state of the current run — the queue, the seats, the register, and the log — plus the generation counter (a single number that increments each time a week is completed, used only to seed the next week's deal), so a week in progress survives closing the page. It contains no personal information: no names, no identifiers, nothing about how you played beyond the run itself. Erasing the saved run below also resets the generation counter to G1.

The Volumen grant is redeemed separately at edit.phext.io, where accounts exist; this page creates no account and sends nothing there. If the hub issues a claim code when all 18 roads are finished, that code is random, generated on the device, and not derived from you, your device, or your play history — it identifies no one.
