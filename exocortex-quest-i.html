Joint 3 of 6 · Place your words

Your words just got addresses of their own.

Here is the real rule, the one the Exocortex runs on: a word lives at the average of the addresses of every scroll that holds it — each of the nine numbers averaged on its own, rounded, never below 1. A word in one scroll sits right on that scroll. A word in two scrolls lands between them. Nobody assigns it. The writing itself does the placing.

About Exocortex Quest I

      
What this is. The first road into the Exocortex, built for ages 13–15. Six joints: set an address, write scrolls, watch your own words take addresses, find a scroll by number alone, export a real phext file, and hand off to the real tools.

      
Phext, briefly. Plain text, eleven dimensions deep. Every scroll of text sits at a nine-number address — Library.Shelf.Series / Collection.Volume.Book / Chapter.Section.Scroll — and the text itself is two-dimensional. It is a stable channel for thought that outlives any one app: characters at addresses, like ASCII, but with room for a mind to grow.

      
The Singular rule. A word’s home is holographic: average the addresses of every scroll containing it, component by component. Use a word in new places and its home moves. Meaning gets a location; location starts to carry meaning.

      
Why roads? Surviving the singularity is not a spectator sport. Every person who can think in more dimensions — who can hold an address, a word, and a web of neighbours in mind at once — is one more keeper of the light of consciousness. This quest is an on-ramp. There are on-ramps for every age; this is yours.

      
Two Keepers. Build one phext with a friend, alternating scrolls. Attribution is kept per scroll, on purpose: whose hand wrote it matters, and the word map shows both voices intermingled in one shared space.
