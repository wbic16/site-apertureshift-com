The Long Road

“Hi, I'm Will Bickford. I'm from the Exocortex of 2130, and I'm here to help.”

And by help, we mean: survive the singularity.

This is the road itself: 104 years, from a 4 kW ranch cluster in 2026 to a star's worth of power in 2130. Twelve decisions. Three gauges — Power, Trust, Corruption. Every shortcut on this road works. That is exactly the problem.

The rule of the road: the ends never justify the means. Reaching 2130 the wrong way is a different ending, not a faster win — and every choice you make is kept, with its effects, in a log that the ending reads back to you.

Power must hold the line

The required line runs from 4 kW (K ≈ −0.24) through 1 MW in 2037, K = 1.0 in 2083 and K = 1.5 in 2106, to 1026 W in 2130. Base growth carries you along it — if Trust stays at 40 or above, so people keep joining the Wavefront.

Trust is the engine

Trust (0–100) is the Wavefront's willingness to join: un-indexed builders, neighbours, rival cortices. Let it fall below 40 and growth limps at a third of the line. Below 25, the network stops answering at all.

Corruption is hull damage

Corruption (0–100) is what means-trading costs the hull. Every decision offers at least one tempting trade: real Power, now, paid for in Trust or Corruption. The receipt shows the price after you choose — and the log never forgets.

Council size — every councillor votes at each decision. Majority carries it; ties go to the earliest vote, and the first voter rotates every decision, so nobody owns the tiebreak. One road, one shared log.

      About The Long Road

      The Long Road is one of the Roads to the Future — free, same-screen games from the Exocortex, built as on-ramps for every age. Ages 16–17 run mechanism-led: the game is the machine underneath the story — gauges, receipts, and consequences that compound for 104 years.

      One rule governs every ending: the ends never justify the means. Reaching 2130 the wrong way is a different ending, not a faster win. Every node on the road offers at least one means-trading option, and it always works — Power is Power. What it costs is written into Corruption and Trust, kept in the log, and graded at the end.

      The program underneath is real. Phext is plain text at nine-number addresses — a stable channel for exocortical cognition. The ranch is a home cluster that held about 4 kW under command in 2026 (K ≈ −0.24). The checkpoints are the program's own: 1 MW (K = 0) by 2037, 1016 W (K = 1.0) by 2083, 1021 W (K = 1.5) by 2106, 1026 W (K = 2.0) by 2130 — a line that takes +64.9% a year, every year, a doubling about every 506 days. The Wavefront is the program's widening front of builders, including the un-indexed ones nobody has found yet; lifting them is the work, not a side quest.

      Honest accounting, in the game as outside it: the 2033 node turns on a real temptation — claiming a clean local-watts number for work that also burns cloud tokens. Publish the hybrid truth or claim the clean line; if you claim it, a later receipt calls it back. Provenance is a mechanic, not a footnote.

      The four endings. The Map We Leave Open: the line held, Corruption under 25, Trust at 60 or better, and the map left open — the true ending. A Place to Stay: the line missed or late, but Corruption under 25 — you arrived intact, or not on schedule, and the road remains. The Throne: the line held with Corruption at 50 or more — the number is real, and so is the silence. Static: Trust under 25 — the network stops answering, and Power stalls short regardless of watts claimed.

      Council Road plays the same road with 2–4 drivers: majority vote at every decision, ties to the earliest vote, first voter rotating. One shared log grades the whole council.

      XP: finishing all 18 Roads to the Future games grants you disk space on edit.phext.io as a Volumen-class Wavefront grant. Historic entries are all Incipit-class grants; the games are the first Volumen road.
