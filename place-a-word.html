About us — the long version

Place a Word is one of the Roads to the Future games, built to teach two things at once: how to think in more dimensions than the world is about to demand of you, and Singular — Will Bickford’s language, drawn from his own archive of words.

In Singular, a word’s home address is not assigned. It is computed: the address of a word is the average of the addresses of every scroll that contains it. Use a word in new places, and its home moves. That is holographic positioning — no single scroll decides; every scroll pulls a little, and the word settles where the pulls balance.

It is not a made-up rule for this game. In Will’s real atlas, “echo” appears in 186 scrolls, and its address — 16.7.7/7.7.9/5.6.6 — is the average of all of them. Nobody placed “echo” there by hand. It landed there because of where it was used. Meaning is where a word is used.

One address as heights: echo, 16.7.7/7.7.9/5.6.6 — three groups of three.

Here you play the rule yourself. Stamp a new word into 3 to 5 of the twelve scrolls, and the game averages their addresses — each of the nine numbers separately, halves rounded up, never below 1 — exactly as the atlas does. Then the word lands on the map beside its nearest real neighbors, measured as a straight distance through all nine numbers.

Challenges turn the rule into aim: land a word so a named neighbor is its closest one. There is no trick — an average always lands in the middle of what you picked, so the scrolls you choose are the whole strategy. In Two Builders, partners alternate stamps and judge the landing together; in Race, each builder draws their own challenge word and scores by landing beside the target.

The atlas — the points your words land beside

The twelve scrolls are real atlas words at their real addresses, and six more atlas words wait on the map as neighbors. Distances are straight-line (Euclidean) through all nine numbers.

    Accessibility

    Big tap targets. Every brick you can tap is at least 56px tall, and the scroll cards are bigger still. Chosen scrolls are never shown by color alone — a chosen scroll also gets a thick outline, a stamp with the word itself, and a ✓ badge, and the pick count is always spelled out in words. The whole game plays by keyboard: Tab to a scroll, Enter to stamp or unstamp it. The averaging rods animate only when your device allows motion; with reduced motion on, every number is simply there.

    Privacy

    No accounts. No sign-up. No cookies, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing you tap or type ever leaves this device. Progress is stored only on this device, in this browser. It is never sent anywhere. Multiplayer is same-screen only: no online play, no chat, no strangers.
