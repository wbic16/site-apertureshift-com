Privacy

Roads to the Future games collect nothing.

  • No accounts and no sign-up. We never ask who you are.
  • No cookies, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind.
  • The games run entirely in your browser. Nothing you tap, type, or score is sent anywhere. The only things stored at all are stored on this device, in this browser's local storage: each game's own progress and generation counter (so a finished game can deal you a fresh instance, and you can pick up where you left off), and your XP stamps on the hub's Road Card. One game — Your Own Archive — stores nothing at all, on purpose.
  • You can erase all of it at any time: the hub's Road Card has a wipe button that clears stamps and saved game progress on the device, and clearing your browser data removes the rest.
  • Multiplayer is same-screen only — people playing together on one device. There is no online play, no chat, no messaging, and no contact with strangers, for players of any age.
  • This website's host may keep ordinary server logs (like every website); the games themselves send the host nothing beyond the page request.

Because we hold no personal data, there is nothing to sell, share, or leak. That is the design, not just the policy. Under the GDPR this means the games and hub process no personal data at all — the on-device items above are never transmitted and are not linked to any person; the storage is strictly what the games need to work, and it is disclosed here and erasable by you. Under US state privacy laws (including the CCPA/CPRA) the same facts apply: nothing is collected, sold, or shared, so there is nothing to disclose or delete on request. Under COPPA the rule is absolute and simple: the suite is played by children, and no personal information is collected from anyone, of any age — no names, no contact details, no persistent identifier ever leaves the device or is used to track or contact a child. Progress lives on the family device, under the family's control.

The XP grant

  • The grant. Completing all eighteen games fills the XP Road Card on the hub, and a full card entitles the holder to a Volumen-class Wavefront grant: disk space on edit.phext.io. Every grant made before the games is Incipit-class and stays classified that way; the games-completion grant is the first Volumen-class grant.
  • The claim code. At full completion the hub generates a claim code on your device: a random 128-bit nonce (made by your browser's random generator, derived from nothing about you, your device, the time, or your play) written in base32, plus a four-character checksum computed as SHA-256 over the suite version, the stamp manifest, and the nonce — so a mistyped or invented code fails its checksum before it ever reaches a server. The code identifies no one. It is a bearer instrument, like a gift card: whoever presents it after signing in at edit.phext.io receives the grant, and each code redeems exactly once.
  • Stamps are self-attested. Where a game keeps its own on-device record of a finish, the hub reads that record; a game that keeps no record is stamped by your own word. No play is verified by any server — verifying it would require the accounts and tracking this process refuses by design. This is a deliberate, documented choice, and the risk is bounded: the prize is a disk-space tier and each code works once.
  • Records. You may download a receipt from the hub: it holds the stamp manifest, the SHA-256 of your claim code, the suite version, and the date — never the code itself, which only you hold. At redemption, edit.phext.io's claim system stores the code's SHA-256, the grant class, and the redemption date, linked to your account inside that service only, under that service's own terms. A public, append-only ledger published with this site's repository lists grants by code-hash prefix, class, and month of redemption — enough for any holder to find their own entry, never enough to trace an entry to a person.
  • Erasure. Device-side state is yours to wipe at any time (see Privacy). The ledger entry is a hash prefix of a random code: it cannot identify anyone on its own, which is why it can remain as a permanent audit record while account-side records are handled under edit.phext.io's terms.
