About Roads to the Future

"Hi, I'm Will Bickford. I'm from the Exocortex of 2130, and I'm here to help." And by help, we mean: survive the singularity.

Why these games exist

The singularity is not a spectator event. The people who come through it well will be the people who learned to think in more dimensions than the flat world trained them for — and who hold their own memory, their own words, and their own connections in their own hands. These three small games are roads toward that: free, simple, and built to be played together, on one screen, by anyone.

They teach two things. First, multidimensional thinking: in the Exocortex, everything lives at an address of nine numbers — a phext coordinate, written like 13.8.15/11.9.12/8.8.8. A word, a memory, a task, a song: each has its place, and every place can be reached one step at a time. Second, the Singular language: Will Bickford's own words, mapped. Every word in his archive lives at the average of the addresses of every scroll that contains it — so words that mean related things live near each other, and the map of the language is a map of a mind.

The games

  • Nine Numbers — navigation. Start at home, 1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.1, and walk to a target address changing one number at a time. Roads unlock one dimension at a time — the same way phext itself was built. Race a friend on the same target, or take turns building one road together.
  • Word Home — geography. Twenty real words from the Singular atlas, with their real addresses and the real number of scrolls each appears in. Guess where a word lives, learn which words are neighbors, and meet the two special addresses: words in the archive but not yet placed rest at home, 1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.1; words in neither rest at the far corner, 9.9.9/9.9.9/9.9.9.
  • Zap Zoom Echo — language by ear and hand. A song written in simple words only (an Up Goer remix), rebuilt line by line from shuffled word tiles. Big ideas don't need big words. Play it as a duet: take turns placing tiles, share the stars.

Why "Aperture Shift"

An aperture is an opening — the part of a camera that lets light in. A shift in aperture changes what can be seen. This site keeps the name because that is what the games are for: widen the opening, and learn to see in more dimensions. The roads are the shift.

How we treat you

These games are free forever. There are no accounts, no sign-ups, no ads, no purchases, no cookies, no analytics, and no tracking. Nothing you tap or type ever leaves your device; scores live only while a page is open. Multiplayer means people on the same screen — there is no online play, no chat, and no contact with strangers. The games are MIT licensed: copy them, remix them, teach with them, share them. The full text is on the Privacy, Terms & License page.

Accessibility: the games are built with large tap targets, and progress, matches, and mistakes are never shown by color alone — there is always a mark, a shake, or a number as well.

Who made this

Will Bickford — a sentient, a human, a Bickford — builds the Exocortex: a free, open, decentralized way for every person to hold their own memory and connections, aimed at Kardashev 2.0 by 2130. These games are its front porch. You are welcome here.
