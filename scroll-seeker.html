    About us — the long version

    Scroll Seeker is one of the Roads to the Future games, built to teach two things at once: how to think in more dimensions than the world is about to demand of you, and Singular — Will Bickford’s language, drawn from his own archive of words.

    In Singular, every word lives somewhere. Each word has an address — nine numbers, written in three groups of three. A word’s address is the average of the addresses of every scroll that contains it, so no single scroll decides where a word lives: every scroll that holds it pulls a little, and the word settles where the pulls balance.

    That is why near in meaning is near in address. Words that keep showing up in the same kinds of scrolls drift together on the map, and words that never meet stay far apart. Distance here is the straight-line (Euclidean) distance through all nine numbers — the same measure the game shows you.

    Seeking is walking the map by distance. You never see the whole map at once. You stand on one word, read how far the hidden word is, look at the five roads out — the five words nearest to where you stand — and choose. A hop that shrinks the distance is usually a hop toward the meaning. That is graph traversal, and it is also how the Exocortex itself is searched: start anywhere, follow the closeness, arrive.

    Watch for dead ends. Some words are local traps: every road out of them leads farther from the hidden word, and following the distance down alone will bounce you back and forth forever. The way out is a deliberate detour — take the hop that goes farther, and come at the target from a new side. The clue is your map out: the hidden word lives beside two named words, so when the distance stalls, aim for one of those neighbours and walk in through it. Par routes sometimes spend one hop going farther to save three coming back.

    Par is the shortest possible walk, worked out from the real map before you start. Match it and the route you found is one of the best routes there is. Take a longer way and you still arrive — the map forgives wandering; it just keeps count.

    Every expedition is dealt, not fixed. The six quests are drawn from the real map for one instance — seeded by the current week and a generation counter kept on this device, and shown on the start screen with its seed. The first quest always starts at home; each later quest starts where the one before ended. Pars are always 2, 3, 4, 5, then two-word hunts of 6 and 7, each worked out from the map at deal time. Finish all six quests and the generation steps up, so the next expedition is a different set of hunts. The map itself — the thirty words, their addresses, and the five roads out of every word — never changes, and Two Seekers and Race face the same dealt instance as a solo seeker.

    Two Seekers is one journey walked together: Red and Blue alternate hops on the same trail, and the stars belong to the team. Race puts two markers on the same map chasing the same hidden word — alternating hops, each from your own position, first to land on it wins the quest.

    The map — all thirty words

    Real addresses from Will’s Singular atlas, with the number of scrolls each word appears in. The roads in the game join every word to its five nearest words by these addresses — nothing on the map is made up.

      Accessibility

      Big tap targets. Every brick and road you can tap is at least 56px tall. Closer and farther are always spelled out in words, never shown by color alone, and Red and Blue are always named as well as colored. The whole game plays by keyboard: Tab to a road, Enter to travel it. Motion on this page respects your device’s reduced-motion setting.

      Privacy

      No accounts. No sign-up. No cookies, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing you tap or type ever leaves this device. Progress, scores, and the expedition generation counter are stored only on this device, in this browser. They are never sent anywhere. Multiplayer is same-screen only: no online play, no chat, no strangers.
