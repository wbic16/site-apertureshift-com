    About Your Own Archive
    
  
    What this is. The 18–20 road of Roads to the Future: the last on-ramp is not a game about Will’s words but a tool for yours. You map a sample of your own writing against his Singular atlas and leave with the seed of your own exocortex — a real phext file built entirely on this device.
    
  
    The Singular method. In Will’s atlas, a word’s address is the average of the addresses of the scrolls containing it, component by component. The atlas was built over his choose-your-own-adventure.phext; this page carries a 90-word sample of it, each word with its earned address and, where the source records it, the number of scrolls it was averaged from. Meaning gets a location; location starts to carry meaning.
    
  
    Unmapped words. A word of yours that appears in none of the atlas sample’s scrolls is not guessed at. By the real placement rule, a word in neither the atlas sample nor the source rests at the far corner: 9.9.9/9.9.9/9.9.9. Your unmapped words are listed in full, resting there, until your own scrolls give them somewhere truer to live.
    
  
    Your voice coordinate. The component-wise average (rounded half-up, floor 1) of the atlas addresses of your distinct mapped words — the same holographic merge used to coordinate tasks in the Exocortex, applied to your vocabulary. The strict reading excludes unmapped words, because the atlas cannot place what it has not read; the far-corner reading counts them in at 9.9.9/9.9.9/9.9.9. Both are shown, both labelled, neither hidden.
    
  
    Two Voices. Two people map in turn on one device. Between voices the page wipes the first sample’s text and keeps only its coordinate and mapped word list — the wipe is a privacy feature, not a loading screen.
    
  
    The tool adjusts. This page is not a frozen answer key. The built-in sample rotates weekly among three; the landmark grounding of the strict reading rotates weekly — 8 of the atlas’s 12 landmark words are dealt in each week, the rest rest out, and the page says which is which; and every analysis is stamped with an instance seed (ISO week plus a per-tab counter, shown as Analysis 2026-W45 · #1) from which the constellation’s dealt dimension and the neighbour readout order are dealt. What never changes: the atlas data itself, the averaging rule, the far-corner rule, and anything derived from your own text. Your words are yours and are never re-dealt.
