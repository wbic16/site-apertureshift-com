About — why addresses and indexes exist

A phext is plain text at addresses. Every scroll lives at a nine-number address, in three groups of three — like 16.7.7/7.7.9/5.6.6, the scroll where “echo” lives. The words and addresses in this game are real: they come from Will's own archive, placed by where those words actually appear.

The lesson, after you have felt it: walking a shelf and opening scrolls one by one costs, on average, half the shelf — N/2 moves. A shelf of 10,000 costs about 5,000 opens. Nobody survives that. But if a scroll has an address, you can jump straight to it in 1 move. And if you keep an index — a list from words to addresses — you can find any word's address fast, at any scale.

How fast is “fast”? Each good yes/no jump halves what is left, so the number of jumps grows like log2 N — painfully slowly:

Scrolls (N)Shelf-walk, averageGood jumps (log2 N)
40≈ 20 opens≈ 5–6 jumps
400≈ 200 opens≈ 9 jumps
2,000≈ 1,000 opens≈ 11 jumps
10,000≈ 5,000 opens≈ 14 jumps

That gap — 5,000 against 14 — is the whole reason the Exocortex gives every scroll an address and every word a home. Search does not get faster by trying harder. It gets faster by changing shape: from a walk, to a jump, to an index.

Every round here holds exactly one scroll containing the target: the target word's own scroll, at its real address. Filler scrolls are generated neighbours, titled with two-word combos from the same vocabulary, so the shelf feels real at scale.
